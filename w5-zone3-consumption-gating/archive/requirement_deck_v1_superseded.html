Zone 3 consumption gating

While a batch is running, a reporting user can read a dataset that was never a valid version — part of tonight's load against part of last night's. Every source object is individually correct, so nothing flags it. The requirement is to put a boundary between loaded and visible: data becomes visible as a controlled publication event, not as a side effect of loading.

Audience  Leadership — decision and direction, not design
Status  Requirement baseline frozen. No solution chosen, and none proposed here

A consumer cannot tell a finished dataset from a half-finished one

The data is not corrupt. It is incomplete in a way that nothing reports.

Why today's controls do not catch it

What exists todayWhat it gives usWhat it misses

A reporting view is doing exactly what it was built to do. Showing the latest state of every object is the problem, because mid-batch the latest state of each object belongs to two different cycles.

The requirement, in two parts

Two different problems, with opposite failure signatures. Separately specified — and jointly delivered.

Part A — publication gating  fails visibly: wrong numbers. Reviewed by the business

One version, or none. A consumer sees a complete version of the data their report depends on — never a partial one, never a mixture of two cycles.
Publication is a decision, not a consequence. New data becomes visible only when publication runs, after every job in the cycle has succeeded. A failed cycle publishes nothing — though the business can still authorise release, on the record.
What is published is the real data. No masked value is ever written to disk as though it were genuine. A dataset failing this is wrong, not merely unprotected.

Part B — governance continuity  fails silently: no error at all. Reviewed by security

Protection travels with the data. A republished object carries the tags, masking and access currently defined for it — including any change governance has made, and never reinstating one it has removed.
Protection lands before anyone can read it. The governance step sits inside the publication gate, not after it.
The two parts are not independent. How much Part B costs is decided by the mechanism chosen for Part A: one that never copies data leaves Part B nearly empty, one that physicalises makes it large.

Two specifications, not two phases and not two workstreams. Options are scored against both together, and Part A cannot go live ahead of Part B.
Binding on both. It has to work on the estate we have: repeatable every cycle, run by the batch identity, and achievable without rewriting existing view logic as new ETL.

What this deliberately does not ask for

A mechanismNo technology named or ruled out
History for usersNo consumer-facing access to earlier versions
Upstream changeHow Zone 2 loads work is untouched
Split for review, joined for delivery — and the Part A mechanism we pick is what sets the size of Part B.

Three units — and why they are kept apart

Two ideas that are easy to merge. Keeping them apart is what keeps the design correct.
Three units
Distinct, and not interchangeable
The object — unit of governance
A table or view. Tags, masking and access bind here. Never at domain level
The consistency group — unit of visibility
The objects one report depends on. Becomes visible together, or not at all
The batch — unit of execution
The jobs that refresh those objects. Publication follows only once all of them succeed
These are two different questions. "Where do the controls live?" is answered by the object. "What has to agree with what?" is answered by the group. Collapsing them — treating the object as the unit of both — produces a design that protects each object correctly and still shows a report two cycles at once.

Views — a working position, not frozen To be tested

A view holds no data of its own; it is a query over the tables beneath it. The working position is that a view is not published itself: it inherits consistency from the published tables it reads, and its dependencies are counted inside the group.

If it holds, the population to gate is the tables beneath the view estate, not the thousands of views themselves. The solution phase confirms or overturns it.

What this means in practice

ForTreatment
A consumption objectA table or view. Carries its own tags, masking and accessGoverned here
A reportDefines which objects must agree — the membership of its groupSets the group
A domainA batch. Not a governed object, holds no tags or policiesExecution only
Every consistency guarantee is only as good as knowing which objects each group contains, and which jobs refresh them. That membership has to be known and kept current as reports and objects change — get it wrong and a report shows two cycles with nothing to flag it.
Protection is set per object, consistency per group — a design that merges the two will fail one of them.

Working positions — not frozen, tested in the solution phase

PositionIf it is wrong
No new completion marker — publication follows the batch's own job dependenciesPublication needs a stored completion record after all
Views inherit; only the tables beneath them are publishedViews have to be gated directly, and the effort on the view estate rises sharply

Publication waits for the slowest job in the groupAccepted, not a defect — but one straggler delays the whole report, so it needs alerting

Parked — real, and not ours

Object ownership and orphan clean-up. Objects created at runtime by a service account, rather than through the deployment path, may be treated as orphans and removed. Owner: platform — Raja / Anoop. Parked, not dropped: it constrains any option that creates objects at runtime, and nothing that only works on objects already deployed, so it stays visible when we compare options.

Still ours, and not parked with it

Whether the batch identity can execute publication, and the governance steps around it, is a requirement on us — separate from whether the resulting objects are legitimate under platform policy. Discussed together; to be tracked apart.
The freeze closes two decisions. The working positions are inputs to the solution phase, to be confirmed or overturned there.

What we need from you

Five calls, none of them technical. One of them narrows the options before we start; the rest block build.

The five calls

The callWhose
May the batch identity read sensitive data in the clear? If no, every mechanism that reads and rewrites the data is ruled out — this one narrows the options before we evaluate them  Answer firstSecurity
Release of an incomplete cycle. Who may authorise publishing a partial dataset, and what record is keptBusiness
The permanent-failure exit. If a job cannot be made to work, does the report stay on yesterday's data, or is partial release invoked — and after how longBusiness
Alerting threshold. How long a report may sit on the previous cycle before someone is told. Without this, the first signal is a user asking why the numbers look staleOps
Retry window. A job fails, is re-run and succeeds — may the batch still publish, however late in the dayOps
The two business calls are one question asked twice: when is yesterday's complete data better than today's partial data? A business judgement, not ours to make.
